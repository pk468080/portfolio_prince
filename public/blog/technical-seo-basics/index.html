Technical SEO Basics: What It Is and What to Check First

By Prince Kumar · 6 November 2026 · 5 min read

Technical SEO sounds complicated, but the idea is simple: it is everything that helps Google find, read and trust your website. Content is what you say. Technical SEO is whether Google can hear it. Here are the basics, in the order I check them.

1. Can Google crawl your site?

Google uses a crawler that visits pages through links, the same way a visitor would. If a page has no links pointing to it, the crawler may never find it. If your robots file blocks important pages by mistake, Google will skip them. I start every technical check in Search Console, looking for crawl errors and pages that Google tried to visit but could not.

2. Are the right pages indexed?

Crawling is finding the page. Indexing is keeping it in Google's library. A page can be crawled but not indexed — usually because Google thinks it has little value, or because it is too similar to another page. Thin pages, duplicate pages and pages with almost no text are the usual reasons. Every important page should be indexed; unimportant pages, like thank-you pages, should not be.

3. Speed: how fast does the page load?

Speed matters for visitors and for Google. The most common causes of a slow site are images that are too large, too many scripts, and pages that are simply too heavy. On one portfolio site I worked on, the homepage was over 800 KB because the photos were embedded directly into the page code. Moving the photos into separate image files cut the page to under 100 KB and made it load far faster. Big images belong in image files, not inside the HTML.

4. Mobile: does it work on a phone?

Google mainly looks at the mobile version of your site now. If text is too small to read, buttons are too close together, or content runs off the side of the screen on a phone, that hurts rankings. Always test on a real phone, not just by shrinking your laptop browser.

5. Sitemap and robots files

A sitemap is a simple list of your pages that tells Google what exists. A robots file tells Google which parts it may visit. Both are small text files, and both should exist. A site without a sitemap forces Google to discover pages only through links, which is slower and less reliable — especially for new pages.

6. Structured data

Structured data is extra code that tells Google what a page is: a person, a business, a product, an article, a FAQ. It helps Google show richer results, like review stars or FAQ answers. But it has to be correct — a missing required field, like the mainEntity on a profile page, will make Google ignore the whole block. I validate structured data every time I add or change it.

7. HTTPS and redirects

Your site should load over HTTPS, and the www and non-www versions should redirect to one single version. Two versions of the same site split your ranking power in half. One version, one HTTPS address, with the others redirecting to it — that is the correct setup.

The order matters: crawling and indexing first, then speed and mobile, then sitemaps and structured data. Content fixes cannot help a page Google cannot reach.
