Google Ads vs Meta Ads: Which One First on a Small Budget?

By Prince Kumar · 6 November 2026 · 5 min read

Small businesses ask me this constantly: should I start with Google Ads or Meta Ads? The honest answer is that they do different jobs, and the right first step depends on what you sell and how people buy it. I run campaigns on both, so here is how I decide.

Google Ads: people who are already looking

On Google, someone types what they need — "plumber near me", "best office furniture Dubai", "SEO agency Gurgaon" — and your ad appears. That person already wants something. Because of that, Google Ads usually brings enquiries faster, and the enquiries are often closer to buying. The trade-off is cost: popular keywords are expensive, and a small budget can disappear in a few clicks if the campaign is not set up tightly.

Google Ads works best when people already search for what you offer, and when one customer is worth enough to justify the click price.

Meta Ads: people who were not looking — yet

On Facebook and Instagram, nobody is searching. Your ad interrupts someone scrolling, so it has to earn attention with a clear image or video and a simple message. Meta is cheaper per click and excellent for reaching a specific audience — by location, age, interests, or behaviour. It works best for products and services people discover rather than search for: food, fashion, home services, local offers, events.

The trade-off is patience. Meta enquiries usually need follow-up before they turn into sales, and you need to test different creatives to find what works.

So which one first?

  • Start with Google Ads if people already search for your service, you serve a local area, and you need enquiries quickly. Keep the keyword list small and the location tight.
  • Start with Meta Ads if your product is visual, your customers need to discover it, or Google clicks in your industry are too expensive for your budget.
  • Run both once one of them is working. Meta can create the demand and Google can catch the people who then go and search for you. That combination is where most of my best results have come from.

Three mistakes I see with small budgets

  • Targeting too wide. A whole country when you serve one city. Narrow location and audience first — you can always widen later.
  • No conversion tracking. If you cannot see which ad brought the enquiry, you are guessing. Set up tracking before spending, not after.
  • Stopping after three days. New campaigns need a few days of data before you judge them. Changing everything daily just resets the learning.
Simple rule: if people search for it, start on Google. If people need to see it first, start on Meta. And whichever you pick, start small, measure every enquiry, and only increase the budget on what is already working.
