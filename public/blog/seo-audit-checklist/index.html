The SEO Audit Checklist I Use on Every Website

By Prince Kumar · 6 November 2026 · 5 min read

Before I change anything on a website, I audit it. An audit is not a report for the sake of a report — it is a list of what is broken, in the order that matters. This is the checklist I run first on any website, whether it is a small business site or a client project. It takes a few hours, and it stops you from guessing.

1. Can Google find and index the pages?

First I check whether the important pages are indexed at all. If a page is not in Google's index, nothing else matters — no title or keyword work will help a page Google cannot see. I check Search Console for indexing errors, look at the sitemap, and confirm the robots file is not blocking anything by mistake. A missing sitemap or a wrong robots rule is one of the most common reasons a new site gets no traffic.

2. Titles and meta descriptions

Every important page needs one clear title and one description, written for a human who will see it in search results. Titles should say what the page is about and include the main keyword naturally. Descriptions should say why someone should click. I also check for duplicates — two pages with the same title are competing with each other.

3. Speed and mobile

Most visitors come from a phone, so I test the site on a phone first, not a laptop. I check how fast the page loads, whether images are too large, and whether buttons and text are easy to tap and read. A slow page loses visitors before they read a single line, and Google notices.

4. Content: does the page answer the search?

For each important page I ask one question: if someone searched for this topic, would this page satisfy them? Thin pages with two lines of text rarely rank. The fix is not stuffing keywords — it is writing the answer properly, with headings, examples, and the details a real person would want. On one project, growing organic traffic by 40% in three months came mostly from this step: on-page fixes plus content written around what people actually search for.

5. Internal links

Pages should link to each other where it helps the visitor. A services page should link to the contact page. A blog article should link to the related service. Internal links help visitors find things, and they help Google understand which pages matter most on the site.

6. Local SEO, if the business serves an area

For a local business, the Google Business Profile matters as much as the website. I check that the name, address, phone number and hours are the same everywhere, that the profile has real photos, and that reviews are being answered. Inconsistent business details across the web confuse both customers and Google.

7. Tracking: are we measuring anything?

Finally, I check that Search Console and Analytics are set up and actually collecting data. Without them, you cannot tell whether any fix worked. An audit without measurement is just an opinion.

The order matters. Fix indexing first, then titles, then speed, then content. Most sites I audit do not need a redesign — they need the basics done properly, in the right order.
