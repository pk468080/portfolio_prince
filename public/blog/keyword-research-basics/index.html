Keyword Research Basics: Find What Your Customers Actually Search

By Prince Kumar · 6 November 2026 · 5 min read

Good SEO starts before you write a single word. It starts with finding out what your customers actually type into Google. That is keyword research, and it is much simpler than the tools make it look. Here is how I do it for a small business.

Start with your customers, not with a tool

Write down the questions customers already ask you — on calls, on WhatsApp, in person. "How much does it cost?", "Do you deliver to my area?", "Which one is best for a small office?" Those real questions are usually the best keywords, because they are written in the customer's own words. Your customers do not search the way marketers talk. They search the way they speak.

Then check what Google suggests

Type your main service into Google and watch what it suggests as you type. Scroll to the bottom for "related searches". Open the "People also ask" questions. All of this is free, and it shows you the exact phrases people use. If Google suggests "SEO agency in Gurgaon" and "SEO services price in India", those are real searches from real people.

Sort keywords into three types

  • Buying keywords. The searcher wants to buy soon: "plumber near me", "office furniture Dubai price", "hire waiter Gurgaon". Few searches, but the people who search are ready. Target these first.
  • Learning keywords. The searcher wants to understand something: "how to improve website speed", "what is local SEO". More searches, less ready to buy — but great for blog articles that build trust.
  • Brand keywords. People searching for your business by name. You should rank for these easily, and they tell you your brand is being noticed.

Pick winnable keywords first

A new website will not rank for "digital marketing" — the biggest companies in the country fight over that word. But it can rank for "digital marketing agency in Najibabad" or "SEO audit checklist for small business". Longer, specific phrases have fewer searches, but far less competition, and the visitors they bring are exactly your customers. Win ten small keywords before chasing one big one.

One keyword, one page

Give each important keyword its own page. If two pages target the same keyword, they compete with each other and neither ranks well. A services page targets the buying keyword. A blog article targets the learning keyword. Keep it that simple.

The test that matters: after a month, check Search Console to see which queries actually brought visitors. The keywords that bring real visitors are the ones to build more content around. Keyword research is not a one-time job — it is a loop: research, write, measure, repeat.
