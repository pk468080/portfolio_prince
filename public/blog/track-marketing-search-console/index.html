How to Track Your Marketing with Search Console and Analytics

By Prince Kumar · 6 November 2026 · 5 min read

Marketing without measurement is guessing. Two free tools from Google tell you almost everything you need to know: Search Console shows how Google sees your site, and Analytics shows what visitors do once they arrive. Here is what to check in each, and what the numbers actually mean.

Search Console: how Google sees you

Search Console is the first tool I open for any website. It is free, and it shows data straight from Google — not estimates. The four things I check every week:

  • Queries: which searches brought visitors, and how many clicks each one got. This is the best keyword research you will ever get, because it is your own real data.
  • Pages: which pages get the clicks, and which pages get shown in search but never clicked. A page with many impressions and few clicks usually needs a better title.
  • Average position: where your pages rank on average. A page at position 11 is one good push away from page one — those are the pages worth improving first.
  • Indexing: which pages Google has indexed and which it has not, plus any errors. New pages should appear here within days; if they do not, something is blocking them.

Analytics: what visitors do

Analytics shows what happens after the click. The numbers I actually use:

  • Where visitors come from: Google search, social media, direct, or ads. If one channel brings most of your enquiries, that is where your effort belongs.
  • Which pages they visit: your most-visited pages are your most important pages. Improve those first.
  • How long they stay: very short visits usually mean the page did not match what the visitor expected.
  • Enquiries: the number that matters most. Traffic is nice; enquiries pay the bills. If you can, track form submissions, WhatsApp clicks and calls as conversions, so you can see which channel actually brings business.

How I use them together

Search Console tells me what to fix. Analytics tells me whether the fix worked. A typical week looks like this: check which queries are rising, improve the pages behind them, then watch the clicks and enquiries over the next two weeks. If clicks rise but enquiries do not, the problem is on the page, not in the traffic. If enquiries rise, keep doing more of whatever caused it.

Three mistakes to avoid

  • Checking daily. Daily numbers jump around for no reason. Weekly or monthly trends are what matter.
  • Chasing traffic instead of enquiries. Ten thousand visitors who never contact you are worth less than a hundred who do.
  • Not setting anything up. The most common problem I find is not bad numbers — it is no numbers at all. Both tools are free, and setting them up takes less than an hour. Do it before you spend anything on ads.
